Every other app in this space matches you on who you are: photos, bios, a personality quiz, a list of interests nobody actually checks. The Field matches you on what you want to do.

Compatibility is a guess. An activity is a fact. The activity is the anchor — the person is a side effect. No algorithm predicts whether two people will get along. The app puts a shared activity in front of both of them and gets out of the way.

The reference scenario: Tuesday, 6pm. Jason, 28, just off work, restless, doesn't want a relationship — just wants out of the house. He filters for "bored now." Eva, 86, grandmother of eight, has a spare ticket to a play and would like company. Jason goes. They have a great night. Nobody owes anybody anything after.

No algorithm made that pairing happen. The activity did the work — and the match ran on bilateral consent, nothing else. Both people agree, at every step. No compatibility score, no "you might also like."

The Field isn't trying to predict whether you'll get along. It's trying to get you both in the room. The rest is yours.

Main Quest. Company-posted events — ticketed or otherwise planned, signup required. This is the only place a company gets any presence in the app at all.

Side Quest. User-planned outings, looking for a match. Bouldering Saturday, a hike, a pickup game, a gallery opening you don't want to walk into alone. This is the core of the app — most of what happens here is Side Quests.

Join Session. Not something you post — a filter across Main Quest and Side Quest. It answers one question: what's actionable right now? Free this afternoon and want in on something today, not next week? That's a Join Session view, not a new kind of post.

Bonus Round. Also not a content type — a tag. Attach it to any Main Quest or Side Quest and it means exactly one thing: whoever posted it is covering the cost. One-directional, always. Never a request for someone else to cover you.

This is Section 8 — Economics. It does not move, regardless of how anyone — founder included — might feel about it later.

$500M personal cap. No employee, no investor, no founder will ever personally make more than this from The Field.

50 / 5 / 30 / 15. How profit splits, always. 50% to The Foundation. 5% to repay investors as fast as possible, fully cashed out, capped. 30% decided democratically by employees each cycle — reinvest it, bonus it out, give it to the Foundation, their call; if taken as cash, it splits completely equally, zero weighting for title or tenure. 15% grows whatever the org decides is worth building next.

Want more money for your specific role? That's base salary, negotiated up front. The bonus pool was never where seniority gets paid.

Automate, then build next. The org should automate itself out of jobs whenever it can, and build the next thing. Growth of headcount isn't the goal — continuous creation of things worth doing is.

This is written down now, before we need it to hold. That's the only time a commitment like this actually means something.

Jobs are being automated out from under people. Third places — the spots that aren't home and aren't work, where you're just around other people with no transaction required — have all but disappeared. People need somewhere to go and something to do together that isn't work and doesn't cost money.

The Foundation runs free local events directly — concerts, city cleanups, whatever a community needs — and can partner with other companies to run bigger ones. It pairs with The Field's parent company but has a purpose independent of it: it would need to exist even if The Field never took off.

It also seeds new companies willing to adopt the same operating structure. Each one sets its own profit split — except a hard floor: minimum 30% of profit to employees, non-negotiable, in every company it seeds.

These are genuinely undecided — left open on purpose. The plan is to find out what early contributors and future users actually want and will tolerate, not to design it alone in a room. Four of the open threads:

Bot / human verification. What mechanism, and what will people actually put up with? We've floated a light paid background check — maybe $10 — but haven't decided. Also open: how people want to report bad actors. (Separate from safety monitoring during real meetups, which is already settled.)

Peer jury structure. When something goes wrong between two people, who decides what happened — a small rotating panel, or a larger pool? Honestly parked right now, not forgotten.

Foundation governance. The most unresolved thread in the whole project. Board structure, grant criteria, how it stays independent of the parent company's commercial incentives — none of it is decided. No legal team yet, and the plan is to crowdfund and build this in public rather than take investor money just to get one.

Map / city-guide scope. It's user-created, not curated top-down — but how far does that go? What should it include, what should it stay out of?
